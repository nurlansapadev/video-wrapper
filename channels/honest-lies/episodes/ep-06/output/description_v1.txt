The chip in your iPhone was made in Taiwan, by one company: TSMC. It started over breakfast in a little Taipei shop in 1974, and the bill was pocket change.
Was it genius, or did Taiwan steal America's chip industry? The paperwork has its own answer.

The story runs from a 1970s Taipei snack shop to RCA's labs in New Jersey, through Morris Chang's years at Texas Instruments, the night Washington switched its recognition to Beijing, and the founding of TSMC in 1987. Along the way: Nvidia, Apple, the silicon shield, and the chip war everyone is talking about now.

How does a breakfast tab buy a country its right to exist?
